# Runner Watch taste audit

## Correct product frame

Runner Watch is a Canadian market-news and commentary product. It does not execute trades, connect to a broker, manage a portfolio, or provide personal recommendations.

Its real medium is a live public argument about the market:

- Pulse shows what is moving.
- Radar shows what changed.
- Alpha shows what the crowd is calling.
- Ticker pages join charts, filings, risk signals, commentary, and later outcomes.
- Flash publishes an AI-generated opinion with linked evidence.

The design direction is a subversive financial newswire with a degen edge. It should feel closer to an independent foreign desk, market zine, and terminal than a careful brokerage app.

This remains a targeted redesign under the [taste-skill framework](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill). It keeps the routes, feature set, public Calls, Flash economy, chart lenses, wolf, and irreverent language.

Taste dials:

- Variance: 6 out of 10. The voice should be recognizable and willing to break polite finance conventions.
- Motion: 2 out of 10. The writing can be wild; the interface should remain steady enough to read.
- Density: 8 out of 10. This is a live news desk, so compression is useful.

## Archetype system

Jung's archetypes are used here as a creative lens, not as a scientific rule about every reader. See Jung's [The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Jung_CollectiveUnconscious.pdf).

Primary archetypes:

- The Sage: follows sources, filings, market facts, and outcomes.
- The Trickster: punctures official stories, names market absurdity, and keeps `Astrology` and `RUG CHECK` alive.
- The Chronicler: records who said what, when they said it, and what happened later.

Atmospheric archetypes:

- The Shadow: asks what the clean company story leaves out.
- The Outlaw: gives the product its foreign, unsanctioned, anti-PR voice.

The Guardian is an editorial standard, not the product's public personality. It appears as sources, timestamps, conflict disclosure, and visible corrections.

## UX psychology

### Information scent

Dense commentary works when each cue tells the reader what kind of material sits behind it. `Filings`, `What could rug it`, `Unknowns`, and `Sources` have useful information scent. A clear `Log in` label is still better than an unexplained arrow. See [Pirolli and Card on information foraging](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_20).

### Recognition and visible status

Live state, publication time, author or model, source links, and unknown values reduce guesswork. These choices follow the usability principles of visible system status, real-world language, and recognition instead of recall. See the [Nielsen Norman Group heuristic summary](https://media.nngroup.com/media/articles/attachments/Heuristic_Summary_compressed.pdf).

### One warning in the right place

Repeated warnings across an editorial product become wallpaper. They also make the whole product look like an execution surface trying to protect itself.

The useful disclosure belongs on the AI report itself:

> Opinions in this report were generated by AI. They may be incomplete or inaccurate and are not financial advice.

The disclosure links directly to the citations and sources below the report. It tells the reader what made the opinion, what can go wrong, and where to check it.

## Canadian context

The Canadian frame is more relevant than the earlier SEC frame. The current joint CSA and CIRO guidance says that purely factual information is not advice, while an opinion about the merits of investing in a business or security can be advice. It also says presentation and promotional language can affect how content is understood.

That does not mean the app needs a disclaimer on every screen. It means the editorial operation should know what kind of statement it is publishing. See [CSA and CIRO Staff Notice 31-369](https://www.ciro.ca/newsroom/publications/joint-canadian-securities-administrators-and-canadian-investment-regulatory-organization-staff).

This document is a product and design audit, not a legal opinion.

## Critical theory critique

### McLuhan

The medium is not a trading terminal. It is a live commentary network. Its message comes from the rhythm of updates, the collision of voices, and the public archive of old Calls.

The interface should therefore make authorship and time legible. It can be fast and provocative without turning every update into a command. See McLuhan's [The Medium Is the Message](https://cast.b-ap.net/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2011/09/mcluhan-MediumistheMessage.pdf).

### Deleuze

Scores and ranks are useful material, but they should not become an oracle. The interesting object is the disagreement between a score, a filing, a chart, a model, and a caller.

Runner Watch is strongest when a number opens an argument instead of closing it. See Deleuze's [Postscript on the Societies of Control](https://cceai.ohrg.org/readings/deleuzePostscriptSocietiesControl1992.pdf).

### Deleuze and Guattari

The product is an assemblage of market bars, SEC filings, news, public Calls, AI writing, pseudonyms, and incentives. Its evidence trail should remain rhizomatic: many paths, many voices, no single approved reading.

The interface should show connections and contradictions without flattening them into one final truth. See the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Deleuze](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/deleuze/).

### Barthes

The wolf, coins, Alpha, Flash, Astrology, and RUG CHECK form a market mythology. In an editorial product, that mythology does not need to be scrubbed away. It can be performed knowingly.

The design task is to stop the myth from pretending to be neutral fact. Clear source links, model attribution, timestamps, outcomes, and visible unknowns let the reader see how the story was made. See Barthes' [Myth Today](https://www.uv.es/~fores/programa/barthes_myth.html).

Barthes' critique of the Author also fits AI reports. Flash is a named voice, not an Author-God. Its opinion sits beside the sources that a reader can use to produce another interpretation.

## Implemented decisions

- Kept the wolf and `#1 most called` as self-aware degen mythology.
- Restored `RUG CHECK`.
- Reframed Astrology as `crowd lore` instead of adding a warning to the chart.
- Kept the bright coin tokens instead of turning them into restrained dossier seals.
- Removed general warnings about popularity, trade alerts, and acting.
- Added one clear AI disclosure near the top of every generated report.
- Linked that disclosure to the report's citation and source index.
- Kept clear login language, keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, and the useful Pulse empty state.
- Kept the chart-first ticker layout and multi-source evidence trail.

## Editorial guardrails

1. Label generated opinion as AI-generated.
2. Link AI opinion to its sources.
3. Keep model name, publication time, and visible unknowns.
4. Correct material errors openly.
5. Disclose payment, holdings, issuer relationships, or other conflicts when they exist.
6. Distinguish reporting, interpretation, satire, community activity, and sponsored material.
7. Do not invent quotes, filings, people, or source support.
8. Preserve strong and strange language. Do not disguise a paid promotion as independent commentary.
9. Treat rankings as news about the crowd, not proof that the crowd is right.
10. Keep an archive so readers can judge the commentary by its record.

## Questions for the next user study

- Do readers understand that a Call is public commentary, not an order?
- Can they tell facts from Flash's interpretation?
- Can they reach the sources from the AI warning without hunting?
- Does the wolf feel self-aware and editorial, or merely promotional?
- Does Astrology read as market folklore rather than a hidden promise?
- Can readers identify the author, model, time, and evidence behind a claim?
- Do the irreverent labels make difficult financial reporting easier to enter without making the facts harder to verify?
